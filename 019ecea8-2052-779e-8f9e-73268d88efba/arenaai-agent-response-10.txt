A few things to clear up:

## 1️⃣ Expo Go on iPhone genuinely has no in-app scanner

I was wrong about that. Apple's policies forced Expo to **remove the in-app QR scanner from iOS Expo Go** in 2023. On **iOS you must use the Camera app** — point it at the QR code, a yellow banner pops up at the top, tap it, and it opens Expo Go. So your original approach (iPhone camera) was correct.

(On Android, Expo Go still has its own scanner. iOS doesn't.)

## 2️⃣ `192.168.1.42` was my placeholder, not your real IP

I used `192.168.1.42` as an **example**. You need to look up your PC's actual Wi-Fi IP first. Open `cmd` and run:

```bash
ipconfig
```

Scroll to the section labelled **"Wireless LAN adapter Wi-Fi"** (not Ethernet, not VirtualBox, not vEthernet, not WSL). Look at the `IPv4 Address. . . . . . . . . . . :` line. It will look something like:

```
Wireless LAN adapter Wi-Fi:
   IPv4 Address. . . . . . . . . . . : 192.168.0.108     ← THIS line
   Subnet Mask . . . . . . . . . . . : 255.255.255.0
   Default Gateway . . . . . . . . . : 192.168.0.1
```

**Paste me the full `ipconfig` output** (you can mask the last digit if you wish) and I'll tell you exactly which IP to use.

## 3️⃣ Verify Metro is actually running while you test

Open **two separate `cmd` windows**:

**Window A** — start Expo and leave it running:
```bash
cd C:\path\to\swiss-round-calculator\mobile
npx expo start --lan --clear
```

Wait until you see the QR code AND a line like:
```
Metro waiting on exp://192.168.x.x:8081
› Web is waiting on http://localhost:8081
```

**Leave that window open.** Metro stops when you close it.

**Window B** — while Window A is still running, test connectivity:
```bash
curl http://localhost:8081/status
```

This should print `packager-status:running`. If it does, the dev server is alive and the next test is the actual LAN IP from `ipconfig`:

```bash
curl http://192.168.x.x:8081/status   ← use YOUR real IP from ipconfig
```

If `localhost` works but the LAN IP fails, the **firewall is blocking inbound connections** even though you saw the prompt — the prompt may have been dismissed for the wrong network profile.

## 4️⃣ Fix the firewall properly (the dialog you saw isn't always enough)

Windows shows that dialog once with **"Private networks" pre-ticked** and **"Public networks" un-ticked**. If your Wi-Fi profile happens to be "Public" (which is the default on a new connection!), the rule doesn't apply.

**One-shot fix in PowerShell — Run as Administrator:**

1. Press Windows key → type `powershell`
2. Right-click "Windows PowerShell" → "Run as administrator"
3. Click "Yes" on the UAC prompt
4. Paste and press Enter:

```powershell
New-NetFirewallRule -DisplayName "Expo Metro" -Direction Inbound -Protocol TCP -LocalPort 8081 -Action Allow -Profile Any
```

The `-Profile Any` is the key — it permits both Private AND Public Wi-Fi profiles.

You can also check / change your Wi-Fi to "Private" so the original firewall rule applies:

1. Settings → Network & Internet → Wi-Fi
2. Click on your connected network name
3. Set **Network profile type** → **Private network**

## 5️⃣ While you're at it: check your Wi-Fi router doesn't isolate clients

Some routers (especially in flats, hotels, dorms) enable "**AP Isolation**" or "**Client Isolation**" which prevents your iPhone from ever reaching your PC even though both are on the same Wi-Fi. Test:

On your iPhone Safari, open: `http://google.com` → should load (proves Internet works).
Then try: `http://192.168.x.x:8081/status` (your PC's real IP) → if this still fails after the firewall fix, AP isolation is on and you'd need to disable it in the router admin page, or fall back to tunnel mode (see step 6).

## 6️⃣ If LAN still doesn't work — switch to ngrok tunnel

The default tunnel (`exp.direct`) is genuinely slow/flaky. ngrok is much better:

```bash
npm install -g @expo/ngrok
npx expo start --tunnel --clear
```

When it asks "Do you want to install @expo/ngrok globally?" → **Yes**. (This is what your "expo@grok" prompt actually was — the spacing in your terminal made it unreadable.)

ngrok normally connects in 5-10 seconds and the bundle loads in another 20-30 seconds.
